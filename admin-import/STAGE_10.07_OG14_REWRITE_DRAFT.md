## 2. Detector hardening (general rules; no resource named)
### 2.1 Target-label duration rule
Flags a fixed supply, storage or preparedness duration set out behind a target label (Target, Goal, Minimum, Required, Aim for, Maintain, Keep at least, Build to) in hours, days, weeks or months, digits or spelled out. Two forms: a **labelled field** (strong label plus colon, directly followed by the duration: "Target: 30 days", no supply noun needed) and a **sentence** (any label with a duration within a short span, only when the sentence is about supplies, storage, preparedness or self-sufficiency: "Build to a 30-day pantry"). Quiet for target dates and deadlines, scheduling words (date, deadline, due, meeting, appointment, project, by, before, until, finish, complete), title-case programme and resource titles ("30-Day Pantry Builder", "90-Day Implementation Roadmap"), a member's blank inside the match, questions, negations and examples.
### 2.2 Assurance / completion claim rule
Flags an unsupported statement that household resilience, safety or preparedness is achieved because a step is complete: "You have secured your water supply", "Your household is now prepared", "You are fully ready for an outage", "Your food resilience is secured", "You now have everything you need", "This guarantees your household is prepared", "guaranteed preparedness", "supply secured". Quiet for questions, instructions ("Write down what you have secured."), conditional or hedged wording (if, once, when, may, can, should, helps), blanks, negations, a bare task-leading "ready" ("You are ready to begin") and ordinary "secure" with no completion context.
### 2.3 Precedence (one statement, one owner)
treatment-owned → registry-approved → safety block → structural label → survival-duration → supply-duration → emergency-period → storage-duration → **target-label duration** → multiplier → comparative performance → absolute outcome → **assurance / completion** → not-a-claim and ordinary numeric rules. A sentence owned by an earlier family raises none of the later candidates (tested: "Maintain 7 days of water" is owned by supply-duration; "Goal: a shelf life of 2 years" by storage-duration; an outcome claim raises no assurance candidate). Documented in the `numeric.ts` header comment.

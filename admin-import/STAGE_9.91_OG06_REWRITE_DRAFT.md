### Page header: OffGrid056 | Emergency Readiness Checklist — General · Planning

### Why This Matters
In a power cut, a storm or another disruption, the things your household needs are much easier to find if you have already thought about them and written down where they are.

This checklist helps you see what your household already has, where it is kept, and what still needs organising. Take it at your own pace. There are no right or wrong answers, and nothing here is a score or a target.

> **BEFORE YOU BEGIN**
> This checklist is for personal planning only. It lists the kinds of things worth having, not how much to keep or what to do in an emergency. Tick what is already in place, leave anything that does not apply to your household, and use the notes to record what you want to organise.

## 13 · Emergency-period detector rule (`admin-import/audit/numeric.ts`)

A third claim family, beside survival-duration (9.86) and supply-duration (9.87): a figure in **hours, days or weeks** (also minutes) is a factual **emergency-period claim**, bucket C_NEEDS_SOURCE ("an emergency-period claim…"), when the wording asserts how long an emergency, a delay in help or household self-sufficiency lasts. It reads eleven patterns: **"the first N hours are critical/crucial/vital/most important/most dangerous"** (either order); **"help / rescue / assistance / emergency services may or can or will (not / take / be) … N days"**; **"survive / get through / cope / manage / get by / be self-sufficient … the worst / first / next N days"**; **"survive / get through / cope / get by / be self-sufficient / manage on your own / prepare to manage … for N days"**; **"without (outside) help / assistance / support / rescue for N days"** (either order); **"the N-hour window / period / standard / rule … critical / self-sufficiency / survival"** (either order); and **"prepare for / be ready for N days"**.

The **supply** family was widened in the same stage (the stage you asked for: "N days of meals", "N-day supply of formula / pet food"): meals, formula, pet food, baby food and snacks are now supply things; "an N-day supply of X" is a target without needing an asserting word (a bare label "14-Day Supply" still is not); "an emergency kit / bag / pack for N days" is a target; and "Plan 7 days of meals" stays ordinary because a planning verb keeps a "days of meals" phrase a menu.

**Context controls it.** The rule stays silent for negated or reassuring sentences, questions, examples ("for example", "such as") and a member's own choice or blank ("write", "choose", "____"), and it names no document or legacy phrase (a test asserts this). Titles, programme names, schedules, completion times, deadlines, plans and roadmap names contain no asserting pattern.

**Ownership, with no duplicate failure.** The classification chain tries survival, then supply, then emergency-period, and stops at the first; a figure is a single candidate keyed by sentence and figure, so a statement two rules could read is one failure with one owner. "Help may not reach you for 3 days without water" is a survival finding; "Keep enough food for 7 days" is a supply finding; "Help may not reach you for 3 days" is an emergency-period finding. The existing rules (ordinary numeric, survival, supply) and every sourced baseline are unchanged and still tested.

**Result on the legacy OG-06:** Bucket C rises from 6 to **12** in each market. The six statements Stage 9.90 reported as missed are now caught: "The first 72 hours are the most critical" · "survive … through the worst 3 days without outside help" · "Help may not reach you for 3 days" · "the 72-hour window as the critical self-sufficiency period" · "3 days of meals that need no refrigeration or cooking" · "3-day supply of formula, pet food, or special items".

Two refinements came from testing, not exemptions: the planning-verb filter first included "cooking" and so dropped "3 days of meals that need no … cooking", so it now lists only plan, menu, recipe and batch-cook; and "key" was removed from the "critical" words so "the first 10 minutes cover the key points" stays ordinary.

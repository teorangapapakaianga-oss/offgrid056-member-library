## 3. Storage vs treatment — the distinction that must hold

| | **Storage** (already approved and live) | **Treatment** (this stage) |
|---|---|---|
| Question | how much safe water to keep, and how to keep it safe | how to make water of unknown or unsafe quality drinkable |
| NZ basis | Get Ready: 3 L/person/day, three days; containers; **"If you use collected rain water, make sure that you disinfect it with household bleach"** | Taumata Arowai / Health NZ / MPI / HE10148 / MBIE |
| AU basis | state emergency services (Get Ready Qld 10 L/person for 3 days — example only) | NSW Health, WA Health, NHMRC |
| Block today | `stored-drinking-water` — deliberately stops at storage | **none exists** |

**Finding, and it is the opposite of what Stage 9.41 assumed.** The NZ "5 drops per litre" is **not storage-only
guidance**. Taumata Arowai publishes it as the alternative to boiling **when a Boil Water notice is in place**, MPI
publishes it for **emergency food preparation**, and HE10148 publishes the same ratio (½ tsp / 10 L) under
**"Disinfect unsafe water"** for tank supplies. All three are treatment contexts, all three are NZ, all three are
current.

**What still may not be done with it:** it may not be carried into AU (NSW and WA both publish different ratios, and
WA's depend on bleach strength), it may not be presented as making water safe from *Giardia* or *Cryptosporidium*
(Taumata Arowai says plainly it does not), and it may not be used where chemicals are suspected.

## 5. Chlorination / chemical disinfection

**NZ — verified, and consistent across three agencies:**

- **5 drops of plain, unperfumed household bleach per litre**, or **½ teaspoon per 10 litres**; **stir; stand 30
  minutes** (Taumata Arowai; MPI; HE10148 gives the 10-litre form).
- **Bleach type is part of the instruction:** plain and unscented only; no surfactants ("foam or bubble when shaken"),
  no perfume, no colour, no other additives; **"Words like 'thick', 'gel', 'foam' or 'cleaner' … normally mean there
  are additives"**; if the label is unclear, do not use it (Taumata Arowai; HE10148; MPI).
- **Limits:** "Bleach does not work on … protozoa. Giardia and cryptosporidium…" — so boiled or bottled water is
  preferred (Taumata Arowai); "some parasites are resistant to chlorine" (HE10148).
- Discolouration with bore-water minerals is not dangerous; taste can be improved by chilling or pouring between
  containers (Taumata Arowai).
- **Separate, different ratio, different purpose:** ¼ cup of bleach per 10 L for **rinsing dishes**; ½ tsp per 10 L for
  **scrubbing faecal material off a roof**. These are not drinking-water doses and must never be shown as such.

**AU — no single national ratio exists:**

| Context | Source | Figure |
|---|---|---|
| Boil-water alert, if you cannot boil | **NSW Health** | 2 drops of unscented 4–5% bleach per litre; **4 drops if cloudy**; 30 min at 25 °C, **1 h at 15 °C, 2 h at 5 °C** |
| Emergency treatment | **WA Health** | by strength: 1% → **10** drops/L; 4–6% → **2**; 7–10% → **1**; stand 30 min; calcium hypochlorite method for bulk volumes |
| Tank / system dosing | **NSW Health (PWSG)** | target **0.5–5 mg/L free chlorine** after 30 min; after contamination, 5 mg/L initial = **125 mL 4% bleach / 40 mL 12.5% hypochlorite / 8 g 65% calcium hypochlorite per 1000 L**; turbidity **< 1 NTU** first |

**Rulings this implies:** the AU file may not state a single Australian ratio. If a ratio appears at all it must carry
its state and its bleach strength, and the member must be told to use their own state's advice and the product label.
**The NZ ratio may not appear in the AU file, and the AU ratios may not appear in the NZ file.**

## 6. Filters

**NZ:** MBIE names "using a very fine in-line filter or purifier" as one treatment option — **with no rating, no pore
size and no pathogen claim**. HE10148: filters may remove tastes and smells, harmful bugs, or chemicals, but **"Most
filters will only remove one or two types of impurities"**, so ask the supplier what the device can and cannot do.
Health NZ, for emergencies: **"Do not assume domestic water filters are effective. They can become contaminated."**
**No NZ source gives micron ratings or removal percentages.**

**AU (NSW and WA, both state-level):**

| Filter | Removes | Does **not** remove |
|---|---|---|
| Polypropylene / ceramic cartridge | sediment, bacteria (ceramic: "some bacteria and parasites") | **viruses** |
| Activated carbon | chemicals (iron, manganese, hydrogen sulphide), taste, odour, colour, algal toxins (PWSG) | **bacteria, parasites, viruses** |
| Micro/ultrafiltration membrane (0.1–0.01 µm) | sediment, bacteria; ultrafiltration **may** also remove viruses | — (30 µm pre-filter required) |
| Reverse osmosis (0.001 µm) | microorganisms and most residual chemicals | — (5 µm pre-filter; waste stream; power) |

Plus: **"Not all filters remove harmful microorganisms"** (PWSG); **"Water filters should not be necessary to maintain
microbial, chemical or physical quality of rainwater if catchments and tanks are well maintained"** (NSW rainwater
fact sheet); **"Only a reverse osmosis water filtration system will effectively remove harmful bacteria"** and filters
**"should not be relied upon to disinfect water"** (WA); certification **AS/NZS 4348 or ANSI/NSF 53**, WaterMark, and
AS/NZS 3497 above 150 kPa.

**Ruling this implies:** **no filter may be described as making microbiologically unsafe water safe in either market.**
Micron ratings may appear **only** in the AU file, attributed to NSW Health. The NZ file gets no micron figures at all.

## 7. UV

**AU (state-level, detailed):** effective against most bacteria, viruses and protozoa; **only when the water is clear**
— "Rainwater supplies may need to be filtered to ensure effective UV treatment"; a **20 µm pre-filter** and a **1 µm
second stage** for *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*; intensity sensor with alarm; shut-off on low UV or power loss;
**constant power supply**; lamp and quartz-sleeve maintenance; **no residual disinfectant — "Water that has been
disinfected using UV should be used straight away, not stored in tanks"**; **does not remove chemicals**; WA adds that
**some low-power systems cannot penetrate Cryptosporidium and Giardia**. The only figure published is a **standard's
class**: ANSI/NSF 55 **Class A (40 mJ/cm²)**, alongside AS/NZS 3497 / 4348.

**NZ:** MBIE names "ultraviolet light treatment" as an option. **That is the whole of what NZ publishes for
households.** No dose, no pre-filtration requirement, no maintenance interval was found.

**Rulings this implies:** the NZ file may name UV as an option and say the supplier must specify what the unit does —
and nothing more. The AU file may carry the clarity, power, no-residual and maintenance points with NSW/WA
attribution. **40 mJ/cm² may be quoted only as part of the NSF 55 Class A standard name, never as "the UV dose you
need".** No dose value may be written for NZ.

## 11. Proposed NZ water-treatment safety block

Every sentence carries its source; nothing is matched to the AU block for symmetry.

> **Making water safe to drink — New Zealand**
>
> 1. Rainwater, stream, bore and tank water is not automatically safe to drink. If you want to use it for drinking or
>    other household uses, it has to be treated. *(MBIE)*
> 2. If you are uncertain about the quality of the water, do not drink it. *(Get Ready)*
> 3. Boiling is the simplest and most effective way to kill germs, including *Cryptosporidium*: boil the water for one
>    minute. A jug with an automatic cut-off switch is fine as long as the jug is full — let it turn off by itself and
>    never hold the switch down. *(Health NZ; HE10148)*
> 4. Store cooled boiled water in a clean container with a lid. It is best used within 24 hours, and it can be boiled
>    again. *(Health NZ)*
> 5. If you cannot boil water, add 5 drops of plain, unperfumed household bleach to 1 litre of water — or half a
>    teaspoon to 10 litres — stir, and leave it to stand for 30 minutes before drinking. *(Taumata Arowai; NZ Food
>    Safety; HE10148)*
> 6. Use only plain, unscented bleach. Do not use bleach with perfume, colour, surfactants or other additives, and
>    treat words like "thick", "gel", "foam" or "cleaner" as a sign the product is not suitable. If the label is not
>    clear, do not use it. *(Taumata Arowai; HE10148)*
> 7. Bleach and purification tablets do not work on protozoa such as *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium*. That is why
>    boiled or bottled water is recommended. *(Taumata Arowai)*
> 8. Do not assume a household water filter makes water safe. Most filters only remove one or two types of impurity,
>    and filters can become contaminated — ask the supplier what the device can and cannot do. *(Health NZ; HE10148)*
> 9. If harmful chemicals or toxins could be in the water, boiling will not make it safe. Use bottled or tankered
>    water and follow the advice of your supplier or public health unit. *(Taumata Arowai)*
> 10. For a rainwater system used for drinking, MBIE lists the options as adding chlorine, using a very fine in-line
>     filter or purifier, boiling the water for one minute, and ultraviolet light treatment. You should have drinking
>     water tested annually, and some councils also require periodic testing. *(MBIE)*
> 11. If you are concerned about your water, contact a Health Protection Officer at your local public health unit or
>     an Environmental Health Officer at your council. They can also advise on testing laboratories. *(HE10148)*

## 12. Proposed AU water-treatment safety block

> **Making water safe to drink — Australia**
>
> 1. Rainwater and surface water are not automatically safe to drink. The national Australian Drinking Water
>    Guidelines say surface water or shallow groundwater should not be used as a source of drinking water without
>    treatment. *(NHMRC — national)*
> 2. NSW Health, for example, recommends that people in urban areas use the public water supply for drinking and
>    cooking; check what applies where you live. *(NSW Health — state guidance, not a national rule)*
> 3. NSW Health advises that water from dams, streams and rivers is not recommended as a source of drinking water
>    unless it is filtered and disinfected. *(NSW Health)*
> 4. If water may be contaminated with microorganisms, bring it to a rolling boil — a kettle with an automatic shut-off
>    does this — then let it cool and store it in a clean covered container in the fridge. WA Health advises vigorous
>    boiling for at least one minute. *(NSW Health; WA Health)*
> 5. Bleach dosing is not the same across Australia, and it depends on the strength of the bleach. NSW Health advises
>    2 drops of unscented 4–5% bleach per litre, or 4 drops if the water is cloudy, left to stand 30 minutes — longer
>    in colder water. WA Health gives doses by strength: 10 drops per litre for 1% available chlorine, 2 drops for
>    4–6%, and 1 drop for 7–10%, left 30 minutes. Follow the advice for your state or territory and the instructions on
>    the product. *(NSW Health; WA Health — both state guidance)*
> 6. Not all filters remove harmful microorganisms. NSW Health notes that ceramic and polypropylene cartridges remove
>    sediment and bacteria but not viruses, and that activated carbon filters treat taste, odour and some chemicals but
>    remove no bacteria, parasites or viruses. WA Health says filters should not be relied upon to disinfect water.
>    *(NSW Health; WA Health)*
> 7. If a filter is being used for health reasons, look for the WaterMark or Plumbing Safety Type Test Mark and
>    certification to AS/NZS 4348 or ANSI/NSF 53, and ask the supplier for the contaminant-removal claims and data.
>    *(NSW Health; WA Health)*
> 8. UV disinfection works against most bacteria, viruses and protozoa, but only in clear water — rainwater usually
>    has to be filtered first. A UV unit needs constant power, regular lamp and sleeve maintenance, and leaves no
>    residual disinfectant, so NSW Health advises that UV-treated water be used straight away rather than stored.
>    *(NSW Health; WA Health)*
> 9. Boiling and disinfection do not remove chemicals, blue-green algae toxins or metals. If chemical contamination or
>    an algal bloom is suspected, the water is unsuitable for drinking — use an alternative supply and follow the
>    advice of your state or territory health department. *(NSW Health)*
> 10. NSW Health advises that professional advice be sought for the design and installation of a treatment system, and
>     your local public health unit can help you find a NATA-accredited laboratory for testing. *(NSW Health)*

**Deliberate asymmetries, all of them sourced:** NZ carries a single bleach ratio, AU carries two state ratios and
says so; NZ carries a boil duration, AU carries a method plus one state's duration; AU carries filter and UV detail,
NZ does not; NZ carries an annual testing figure, AU carries none.

## 13. Proposed fail-closed claim gates

To be added to `admin-import/audit/` and driven by a new `admin-import/config/treatment-sources.json` registry (each
entry: market, exact figure, unit, contact time, source id, source date). **A claim passes only by matching a registry
entry for the file's own market.** No keyword-only rules; each gate needs the surrounding sentence.

| Gate | Fires when | Passes only if | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| `UNSOURCED_BLEACH_RATIO` | a quantity + unit (drops / tsp / mL / cup / g) within the same sentence, or 200 characters, of *bleach, chlorine, hypochlorite, disinfect, purif\** | the exact quantity, unit, volume **and** standing time match a registry entry for **this market**, and the visible text names the source (NZ: 5 drops/L, ½ tsp/10 L, 30 min; AU: the NSW and WA entries with their strengths) | block the resource |
| `UNSOURCED_BOIL_TIME` | a duration near *boil* | NZ: "one minute"/"1 minute"; AU: "at least one minute" **with WA attribution**, or a non-numeric rolling boil. Any other duration, any altitude or elevation adjustment, fails | block |
| `UNSOURCED_FILTER_CLAIM` | a filter noun (*filter, cartridge, ceramic, carbon, membrane, micron, µm, reverse osmosis, purifier, gravity*) in a sentence that also carries an efficacy token (*removes, kills, eliminates, safe, purifies, %, pathogen, bacteria, virus, protozoa, giardia, crypto*) | the claim matches a registry statement for this market. **Micron values are AU-only and require NSW attribution. No micron value may appear in an NZ file.** A claim that any filter makes unsafe water safe always fails | block |
| `UNSOURCED_UV_CLAIM` | *UV / ultraviolet* in a sentence with a dose unit (mJ/cm², µW, mW), a percentage, or an absolute safety verb | AU only, matching NSW/WA wording; the 40 mJ/cm² figure is allowed **only** inside the phrase naming ANSI/NSF 55 Class A. In NZ, only "ultraviolet light treatment" as a named option passes | block |
| `UNSOURCED_TESTING_INTERVAL` | an interval (*annually, monthly, weekly, every N months/years*) within a sentence containing *test, testing, sample, laboratory, inspect* | NZ: annual drinking-water testing (MBIE) and annual tank inspection (HE10148). AU: only NSW operator frequencies **with the operator label**. Anything else fails | block |
| `UNSAFE_CONTAMINATED_SOURCE_GUIDANCE` | a contamination token (*sewage, septic, flood, floodwater, chemical, spray, pesticide, fuel, petrol, diesel, dead animal, algae, blue-green, cyanobacteria, saltwater, sea water, heavy metal, lead, unknown source*) in a sentence that also carries a treatment or safety verb (*boil, filter, treat, chlorinate, disinfect, UV, safe to drink, drinkable*) | the same block also contains, from the market's registry, an explicit statement that this treatment is **not** sufficient and that an alternative supply or public-health advice is required. **Absence of that statement is a failure, not a pass** | block |

Two supporting rules: the existing brand rule must catch **"Berkey-style"** in OG-09, and a new
`TREATMENT_WITHOUT_BLOCK` check should fail any resource whose text teaches a treatment method while the
`water-treatment` block for that market is absent.

## 4. Privacy, third-party and security findings (items 15 to 18)
- **Personal data about the member:** only implicit (their own skills and assets offered).
- **Third-party data (the central problem):** the Asset Map asks the member to write, for each neighbouring household, **their address or name, the assets and skills they hold, their "potential needs", their contact method and the relationship status**; the model section adds "what they have (tools, skills, equipment, supplies)" and "what they need (spare water, generator power, medical training, comms)"; the agreements ask for each household's skills and the participants' households. None of this is collected from those people with their knowledge or consent; it is a member's private notes about neighbours, which become a **household-by-household map of addresses, equipment, supplies and needs** if written down, photographed or shared.
- **Security and vulnerability content that must not appear in any future shared or printed sheet:** neighbours' addresses; who holds generators, fuel, tools or supplies; "potential needs" (which can reveal medical or vulnerability information); who is home or away; the member's own spare water, generator power and medical training offered outward; a physical message-drop location; school evacuation procedures. Also not appropriate to print as a fixed field: a radio frequency or channel (UHF, CB or amateur radio, which has licensing implications) and exact emergency check-in times.
- **Future rewrite approach:** use **role and category labels** (for example "a neighbour who could help with tools"), **first names or roles only, opt-in contact details the other person has agreed to share**, and the member's **own offers and needs only**; never record another household's assets, vulnerabilities or address; keep contact details member-controlled and optional. No privacy copy is drafted here.

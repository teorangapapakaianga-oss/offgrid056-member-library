## 6. Recommended AU gas architecture

**Recommendation: option A — a national non-numeric core, with state-specific overrides added only where a
jurisdiction is actually named.** Concretely:

- **The AU body of any gas block carries no number and no leak-switch instruction.** It carries what all eight agree
  on: only a licensed gasfitter may do gas work; appliances need regular servicing; follow the manufacturer's
  instructions; outdoor appliances stay outdoors; and **"the rules differ where you live — check your state or
  territory's energy safety regulator"**, said in member-facing words.
- **Anything beyond that is an explicitly labelled override**, in the shape `fire-and-smoke-alarms` already proved:
  *"Fire and Rescue NSW advises…", "In Queensland it is legislation…"*. A member then reads their own state's rule
  as that state's rule, and never receives another state's by default.
- **Leak response gets no shared Australian sentence at all** until there is a way to ask the member which state
  they are in. Four framings, one of them internally inconsistent, is not something a single sentence can carry.

**Why not the alternatives.** Fully state-specific blocks (B) means eight bodies per block and eight times the
review surface, for a library with no state-level routing — and a member in a state we have not drafted gets
nothing. A national general block plus separate state interval/leak blocks (C) is the same problem wearing a
different hat: the per-state blocks still have to be selected by something, and today nothing selects them.

**The requirement is met by construction:** with no interval and no leak instruction in the AU body, a member
cannot receive another state's. The only way to give them a state's rule is to name that state in the sentence,
which is what the Stage 9.52 `requiresLabel` mechanism already enforces for figures.

**What this costs, said plainly:** the Australian gas wording will be less specific than the New Zealand wording,
permanently, until state routing exists. That is the honest consequence of a federation that does not agree with
itself, and it is better than being confidently wrong in five jurisdictions.

## 14. OG-17 false-positive audit

**Verdict: A — narrative / disruption context only.** This is not a close call.

**Exact gas mentions — all four, which are two sentences counted twice because the audit reads both the HTML and
the PDF:**

1. *"Plan your solid fuel system for when the power goes out and **the gas** stops flowing."* (cover subtitle)
2. *"When the grid fails in winter, electric heaters stop. **Gas** supplies can be disrupted. But a wood burner with
   a dry wood supply keeps burning."* (intro)

**Teaching signals: none, across all eight categories tested** — no appliance named, no fuel named (no LPG, propane,
butane, natural gas), no flued/unflued wording, no servicing instruction, no clearance or distance, no leak
procedure, no licensing instruction, no connection or regulator wording. The document mentions **solid fuel 45
times** and gas twice. It is a wood-burner planner that uses mains gas as an example of something that fails.

**Detector reason:** `safetyExposureFor` on the audit's extracted text returns `solid-fuel-heating`,
**`gas-and-lpg`**, `fire-and-emergency`. The gas requirement comes solely from `/\bgas\b/gi` with `needs: 3` and
**no `teaching` condition** — four bare occurrences of the word. The fuel check `GAS_SAFETY_REQUIRED`, which is
compound-based, returns **none** on OG-17's own HTML. **The two mechanisms disagree, and the keyword one is wrong.**

**Proposed exemption — not created:**

```
resource:        OG-17
block:           gas-and-lpg
reason:          Narrative supply-disruption context only. The document plans solid fuel heating and names
                 mains gas twice, as an example of a supply that fails in an outage. It names no gas appliance
                 or fuel, gives no servicing, clearance, leak, connection or licensing instruction, and carries
                 zero teaching signals across all eight tested categories. 45 solid-fuel mentions against 2 gas.
allowedMentions: ["the gas stops flowing", "Gas supplies can be disrupted"]
```

**Lapse conditions — it must void automatically on any of:**

- any mention of `gas` in the migrated member-facing text other than those two exact phrases (the existing
  `exemptionHolds` rule);
- **any gas teaching signal**, via a new `fuelTeachingSignals` test mirroring `treatmentTeachingSignals` and
  `fireTeachingSignals` — an appliance or fuel named, a servicing interval, a clearance, a leak procedure, a
  connection or regulator, or a licensing instruction;
- any `GAS_SAFETY_REQUIRED` finding from the compound fuel check, which is unaffected by the exemption.

**OG-17's other two required blocks both exist**: `solid-fuel-heating` is approved and live in four resources, and
`fire-and-emergency` is answered by `fire-and-smoke-alarms` through the Stage 9.52 `answers` field. **With this one
exemption, OG-17 would have no unmet safety requirement** — and it would need no gas wording at all.

## 15. Gas detector proposal — design only, no code changed

**Two changes, mirroring the water-treatment pattern that has worked since Stage 9.43.**

**(a) Give the gas topic a `teaching` condition.** Today: `{ pattern: /\bgas\b/gi, block: "gas-and-lpg", needs: 3 }`.
Proposed: the same pattern and threshold, plus a `teaching` regex that must also match before the block is required:

```
appliance or fuel named   LPG | LP gas | natural gas | biogas | propane | butane |
                          gas (heater|heating|cooker|cooktop|hob|stove|oven|fire|fireplace|bottle|
                               cylinder|water heater|hot water|barbecue|bbq|lamp|lantern|burner|fridge) |
                          cabinet heater | patio heater | camping (stove|cooker) | unflued | flued | dual-fuel
instruction given         servic\w+ | check\w* | inspect\w* | install\w* | connect\w* | replace\w* | turn off
                          …within 80 characters of a gas or LPG word
figure attached           an interval, a clearance, a cylinder size or a test date beside a gas word
leak procedure            smell gas | gas leak | soapy water | bubbles | ignition source
licensing instruction     gasfitter | gas fitter | licensed gas worker | certificate of compliance
```

**Coverage against your list:** gas heater ✅ · LPG ✅ · propane ✅ (new) · butane ✅ (new) · camping stove ✅ (new) ·
patio heater ✅ (new) · gas cooker ✅ · gas water heater ✅ · cylinder / bottle ✅ *as a gas compound only* ·
gas leak ✅ (new) · gas supply ⚠️ *deliberately excluded* · gas meter ✅ (new, as a compound) · gasfitter ✅ (new) ·
dual-fuel ✅ · biogas ✅.

**(b) Extend the compound fuel check** `GAS_SAFETY_REQUIRED` with the same new terms — `patio heater`, `butane`,
`propane`, `camping stove`, `camping cooker`, `gas leak`, `gas meter`, `gas line`, `gasfitter`, `mains gas`,
`reticulated gas`, `LCC27`, `POL valve` — so a resource that teaches gas without ever writing the bare word is still
stopped.

**False-positive risks.**

- **"gas supply" is the OG-17 trap**, so it stays out of the *teaching* signal. A sentence about mains gas failing
  in an outage is a narrative premise, not gas teaching. It remains inside the topic *mention* count, which is what
  an exemption is for.
- **Bare `cylinder` and `regulator` must stay out** — OG-10 and OG-B08 are full of water tanks and OG-18/OG-19 of
  electrical regulators.
- **"venting gas"** in the approved battery block and **"greenhouse gas"** in any future energy content: both safe,
  because the compound design never matches a bare `gas`.
- **`flued`** would catch "unflued wood burner", which is solid fuel, not gas. Acceptable: it would pull in a block
  that resource should probably carry anyway, and the owner sees it in review.
- **`gasfitter`** appears in the `general-disclaimer`, which is on all 20 resources — so this term must be applied
  to the resource's **own** text only, exactly as the fuel check already is. If it were ever applied post-injection,
  every resource in the library would demand a gas block.

**False-negative risks.**

- A resource that teaches gas **entirely through a product table** — "Flued gas | $1,500–$4,000 | 80–90%" — scores
  low on instruction verbs. **This is OG-B09.** The appliance-named signal catches it, but it is worth stating that
  table-shaped teaching is the weak spot, as it was for water treatment until `tableClaims` was added.
- Brand names ("Rinnai", "Bromic") with no generic term would be missed entirely. No allow-list can fix that; owner
  review is the control.
- A member-facing instruction written only as "have it serviced" with the appliance named two sentences earlier
  would fall outside the 80-character window. Widening it trades false negatives for false positives; I would keep
  80 and rely on the appliance-named signal.

**Nothing was implemented.** No detector code, no exemption, no block.

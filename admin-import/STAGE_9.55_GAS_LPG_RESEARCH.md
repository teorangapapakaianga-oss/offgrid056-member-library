> **Headline finding.** There is **no national Australian household gas rule** on any of the three things a member
> would most want a number for — servicing interval, cylinder test interval, or hose replacement. NSW says service
> annually, Victoria says every two years, WA says every two years *unless the appliance is over ten years old, then
> annually*, and Queensland gives no interval at all. **Two jurisdictions directly contradict each other on what to
> do in a gas leak.** Any block that states one Australian rule would be wrong in at least five jurisdictions.

**Exact supported claims, New Zealand.**

- **Licensing (NZ-5):** "Have any gasfitting done by a currently licensed gasfitter or certifying gasfitter. This includes installation of pipework, gas appliances and flues, and any additions and alterations. Always ask to see their current practising licence ID card." All gasfitting since 1 July 2013 requires certification: a **Gas Safety Certificate** within **20 days** of connection, and **Certificates of Compliance** for high- and general-risk work; records kept **at least seven years**.
- **Servicing (NZ-5):** "flame effect heaters and LPG cabinet heaters should be serviced annually"; "other types of space heaters and water heaters should be serviced at least every two years". NZ-6 states the general case as "having appliances serviced at least every other year". NZ-9 (unflued heaters): "serviced by a qualified person at least once a year".
- **Unflued heaters (NZ-2, NZ-9):** "Don't use unflued gas appliances, such as LPG cabinet heaters or instantaneous water heaters in small rooms or in bedrooms and bathrooms." "The room needs to be well ventilated. Keep internal doors and at least 1 window open to allow fresh air to enter the room. Check that room vents are not blocked. Never use an unflued gas heater in the room where you sleep, in a bathroom, caravan or tent." "Do not do any maintenance on an unflued gas heater yourself."
- **Clearance (NZ-3):** the "Heater Metre Rule" — "Always keep at least one metre from any heat source"; "Keep heaters at least one metre away from materials that can burn".
- **Outdoor appliances (NZ-2, NZ-6):** "Outdoor gas appliances should never be used indoors. This includes patio heaters, barbecues and camping cookers." NZ-6 gives the reason: they lack the oxygen-depletion shut-off systems that indoor appliances have.
- **Leak test (NZ-3):** soapy water on the cylinder connections after refilling; bubbles mean a leak; close the valve and contact the LPG service agent.
- **Leak response (NZ-4), indoors:** keep flames and cigarettes away; **"Never operate any electrical switches when a gas leak is suspected"**; don't use a mobile phone in the area; turn off valves to appliances if safe; turn off the supply at the meter or cylinder; open doors and windows; if the smell persists, go outside. **Outdoors:** keep flames, cigarettes, vehicles and electrical equipment away; turn off at the meter or cylinder; leave the area. Emergency: **111**.
- **Cylinder transport (NZ-7):** good condition; **secured upright** to prevent liquid leakage ("even more dangerous than a gas leak"); valves safeguarded; not connected to an appliance in transit; no smoking; not left unattended in a vehicle, "particularly during hot weather".
- **Camping appliances (NZ-8):** "the majority of deaths associated with the use of camping appliances have been attributed to carbon monoxide poisoning"; use outside whenever possible; never in confined spaces such as small tents; ventilation "should allow air to move across the space with openings at high and low levels".

**Not found in New Zealand:** a household LPG **cylinder storage** specification (distance, enclosure or orientation at home — only transport is covered), a **hose or regulator replacement interval**, a **cylinder test interval**, a **CO alarm lifespan, placement distance or test interval**, and any ventilation figure in m³/h or air changes.

**Exact supported claims, by jurisdiction.**

- **VIC (ESV):** "Energy Safe Victoria and the Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC) recommend that all gas water heaters, space heaters and central heaters are serviced **once every two years** by a qualified gasfitter." A gasfitter "must use a Carbon Monoxide (CO) analyser to test for carbon monoxide spillage". "Never use an oven to heat a room or as a clothes dryer." "Turn off your radiant gas heater when you leave the house or go to sleep – never leave your gas heating on overnight." Do not pour or fill petrol near a gas appliance or flue outlet; do not spray aerosols near operating gas appliances.
- **NSW:** "all gas installations must be carried out by a licensed gasfitter". Refillable LPG cylinders "can only be reused if they have a current, legible test mark from a certified gas cylinder test station of **no more than 10 or 15 years old**". Appliances sold must be marked with the gas type, approved by a registered certifier, and carry an Australian recognised certification label. LPG "concentrations as low as **two per cent** can ignite the air", natural gas "just over **five per cent**". **Leak response:** "turn off your gas appliances; open all doors and windows; **turn off your gas cylinder or gas supply and isolate your power supply**; evacuate the area." Explosions have occurred where cylinders were left in closed vehicles.
- **QLD:** "Only a licensed gasfitter can carry out gas work… Always ask to see a person's gas work licence." Appliances "should be periodically checked and serviced by a licensed gasfitter" — **no interval is given**. Cylinders: keep away from heat and direct sunlight; **carry and store upright at all times**; POL valves need a valve plug (and for 9 kg or less when transporting); LCC27 needs a dust cap; do not use a corroded or damaged cylinder; the test date stamped on base or neck is **valid for 10 years**; do not refill your own cylinder; only a trained and competent person may repair or remove cylinder valves. **Leak check:** detergent and water sprayed on fittings, hose, regulator and connection point; bubbles mean a leak. **Leak response:** "don't panic, don't light any fires, and **don't turn any electrical devices (light switches) on or off**; turn off the gas appliance; open doors and windows; if you can still smell gas, turn off your gas supply and contact your gas supplier or a gasfitter; if you can still smell gas after turning off your gas supply, **call 000**." For fire, explosion or uncontrolled leak: call 000 immediately and evacuate.
- **SA:** "Cylinders need to be safety tested or replaced **every 10 years**", stamped with T plus month and year on the collar, neck or foot ring. "Hoses and regulators should also be certified to Australian Standards… **Hoses should be replaced every five years**." Store cylinders **upright in a well ventilated outdoor area**, protected from excess heat and ignition sources, with a sealing plug in the valve when disconnected. Use LPG cylinders only with LPG appliances. "Dew lines around the cylinder when an appliance is running are common and not dangerous. Ice build-up indicates more gas is needed than the cylinder can provide."
- **WA:** LPG "expands to **270 times its volume**" and is "about **1.5 times heavier than air**". "Cylinders must never be laid down. Always keep cylinders upright even during transportation, when they must be well secured." "Ensure that gas appliances are only operated in adequately ventilated spaces." "When any leakage from the cylinder valve is detected, move the cylinder to a safe location **at least 20 metres** from any sources of ignition." "Dry chemical fire extinguishers are suitable for LP Gas fires." The Director of Energy Safety has **prohibited the sale, hire and use of POL-to-LCC27 adaptors** in WA; POL valves are being phased out over a **10-year period** from 1 April 2021. Servicing (search only, to re-read): "at least every two years… **If the appliance is more than 10 years old, it should be serviced annually**."

## 5. Portable LPG appliance findings

- **Cabinet heaters (NZ):** not recommended in homes with people with respiratory conditions; one-metre clearance in every direction; fireguard where there are children; **never in any bedroom, bathroom or confined space**; keep a window open; soapy-water leak test after every cylinder change; **serviced every 12 months**; store seasonally used portable LPG appliances **under cover** so debris, insects or spiders cannot enter the burner area; since **1 April 2011** new cabinet heaters must be approved by WorkSafe before sale, certified to the European cabinet heater standard and perform satisfactorily on New Zealand LPG.
- **Camping appliances (NZ):** most NZ deaths involving camping appliances are CO poisoning; use outside whenever possible; **never in confined spaces such as small tents**; if inside, plenty of space and ventilation "with openings at high and low levels"; flame should touch no more than the bottom of the pot; stop using any appliance that smells or malfunctions.
- **Barbecues and outdoor heaters:** NZ forbids indoor use outright (patio heaters, barbecues, camping cookers). QLD treats barbecues as approved gas devices with manufacturer instructions. WA's whole cylinder programme is built around barbecue safety (LCC27, adaptor ban).
- **Appliance certification:** QLD "national gas device approval badge", flame failure devices, automatic reignition. NSW: marked with gas type, approved by a registered certifier, Australian recognised certification label. NZ: cabinet heaters approved by WorkSafe before sale.
- **Emergency indoor use:** **no source in either market offers any**, and none will be written. The only statements found are prohibitions. **No improvisation guidance exists to quote, and none may be invented** — this is the clearest "say less" case in the whole research.

## 7. Hose, regulator and connection findings

- **Replacement interval:** **only South Australia publishes one** — "Hoses should be replaced every five years."
  No other jurisdiction read, and no New Zealand source, publishes a hose or regulator replacement interval.
  **Any legacy five-year or two-year hose rule applied nationally would be unsupported.**
- **Certification (SA):** "Hoses and regulators should also be certified to Australian Standards and have a
  certification number printed on them from recognised certifying body."
- **Correct regulator (QLD):** "use the approved regulator supplied with the device… ask your gas supplier or the
  manufacturer to explain how."
- **Seals (QLD):** "make sure that the rubber seal is intact and in good condition. Do not connect or use with
  damaged or missing seals."
- **Condition (NZ, FENZ — search only):** "Your gas hose shows no sign of damage or wear."
- **Connection types:** the 2021 POL → LCC27 change is national in origin but **enforced differently**: SA explains
  compatibility, WA has **prohibited adaptors by order**, QLD describes both valve types. A member in WA and a
  member in SA are not under the same rule.
- **DIY limits:** every jurisdiction read says gasfitting work must be done by a licensed gasfitter. QLD and SA
  additionally forbid repairing or removing **cylinder valves** yourself. NZ requires a **certifying gasfitter** to
  turn the gas back on after any interruption, because the piping may have filled with air and need purging.

**The only DIY action any official source endorses is the soapy-water or detergent leak check** (NZ, QLD). Nothing
else — no tightening, no replacing, no relighting pilots after an interruption.

## 8. Leak response findings — **the jurisdictions contradict each other**

| Action | NZ (WorkSafe) | NSW | QLD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical switches | **"Never operate any electrical switches"** — a flick could spark | **"isolate your power supply"** | **"don't turn any electrical devices (light switches) on or off"** |
| Mobile phones | "Don't use your mobile phone in the area" | — | — |
| Flames / cigarettes | keep out of the room and away | don't use lighters or matches | don't light any fires |
| Turn off appliances | yes, if it can be done safely | yes | yes |
| Turn off supply | at the meter or LPG cylinder | at the cylinder or supply | if the smell persists |
| Ventilate | open doors and windows | open all doors and windows | open doors and windows |
| Evacuate | if the smell persists, go outside to a safe place | **evacuate the area** | evacuate if there is a fire, explosion or uncontrolled leak |
| Who to call | gas retailer for supply; **licensed gas worker** for installation or appliances; **111** in an emergency | gas supplier or licensed gasfitter | gas supplier or gasfitter; **000** if the smell persists after the supply is off, or for any fire or uncontrolled leak |

**NSW's "isolate your power supply" and Queensland's "don't turn any electrical devices on or off" cannot both be
written as Australian advice.** They are not a wording difference; following one while believing the other is how
someone creates a spark. This single row is the strongest argument in this report for a jurisdiction-labelled block
and against any "Australia" block.

**New Zealand's rule is unambiguous and is the safer of the two framings**, but it is New Zealand's and may not be
exported.

## 12. Detector audit

**Two mechanisms currently stop gas content, and they behave very differently.**

**(a) The fuel check — `GAS_SAFETY_REQUIRED`** (`pilot/prep.ts`), run on the resource's **own** text before blocks
are injected. Compound-term based:

`LPG | LP gas | natural gas | biogas | gas-powered | gas-fuelled | gas-fired | dual-fuel | tri-fuel | unflued | cabinet heater | gas-(heater|heating|appliance|cooker|cooktop|hob|stove|oven|fire|fireplace|bottle|cylinder|water heater|hot water|barbecue|bbq|lamp|lantern|burner|ring|fridge|refrigerator)`

| | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Good on appliances and fuels. Correctly catches every item in your scope list that is written as a gas compound. Correctly ignores block wording, because it runs pre-injection — which is why the CO block's "gas heaters" does not fail OG-13 |
| **False-positive risk** | **Low, and deliberately so.** The compound design avoids "greenhouse gas", "blood gas", "venting gas" (which appears in the approved battery block). One mild risk: `\bunflued\b` alone would catch "unflued wood burner", which is not a gas appliance |
| **False-negative risk** | **Real, and worth fixing before any gas resource is migrated.** Not caught today: **"patio heater"** (a gas appliance our own approved block names), **"butane"**, **"propane"**, **camping stove / camping cooker**, **"gas leak"**, **"gas supply"**, **"gas line"**, **"gas meter"**, **"gasfitter"**, **"mains gas"**, **"reticulated gas"**, **"flued"**, a bare **"cylinder"** or **"regulator"**, **"POL"** / **"LCC27"**, and **"9 kg bottle"** |
| **Recommended future architecture** | Keep the compound list and **add**: `patio heater`, `butane`, `propane`, `camping (stove|cooker)`, `gas (leak|supply|line|meter|bottle)`, `gasfitter`, `mains gas`, `reticulated gas`, `LCC27`, `POL valve`. Keep `cylinder` and `regulator` **out** unless qualified, or OG-18's battery and OG-10's tank content will trip |

**(b) The topic detector — `SAFETY_TOPICS`** (`audit/group-a.ts`): `{ pattern: /\bgas\b/gi, block: "gas-and-lpg", needs: 3 }`.

| | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Broad to the point of bluntness. Any three uses of the bare word demand a block that does not exist |
| **False-positive risk** | **High, and already realised.** **OG-17** is held by this rule on four narrative uses of "gas supplies can be disrupted". Water-treatment solved exactly this problem with a `teaching` condition; the gas topic has none |
| **False-negative risk** | A resource that teaches gas safety using only compounds — "always use a licensed gasfitter for your patio heater" twice — scores **0** against `\bgas\b` and would not require the block. The fuel check would stop it, but the *block requirement* would not fire |
| **Recommended future architecture** | Give the gas topic a **`teaching` regex**, exactly as water-treatment has: an appliance named with an instruction, a dose, a clearance, a servicing interval, a leak procedure, a connection type. Then "the gas stops flowing" stops demanding a safety block, and "service your gas heater every two years" starts demanding one. **Also add a `fuelTeachingSignals` lapse test** so any future gas exemption self-voids the moment a resource starts instructing |

**No detector code was changed in this stage.**

## 13. Proposed reusable gas/LPG blocks — proposals only

**A fundamental shape decision first.** Every other block in this library is `marketBody: { NZ, AU }`. **Gas cannot
be built that way.** The AU body would have to pick one state's servicing interval, one state's cylinder test
interval, and — fatally — one state's leak-switch rule. **Three options:**

1. **AU body = jurisdiction-labelled paragraphs** (NSW says…, Victoria says…, Queensland says…), as
   `fire-and-smoke-alarms` already does for alarms. Proven, but gas has 8 jurisdictions, not 2.
2. **AU body = the shared floor only** (licensed gasfitter, certificate, manufacturer's instructions, leak
   essentials that all agree on) **plus an explicit instruction to check the member's own state**, with no interval
   and no distance. Shorter, safer, less useful.
3. **Per-jurisdiction market codes** (AU-NSW, AU-VIC…) — a genuine architecture change, and a large one.

**My recommendation is (1) for the heating and leak blocks and (2) for the general block**, but it is your call and
it is the main thing Stage 9.56 should settle.

## 14. Unresolved gaps

1. **TAS, ACT and NT were not read live.** Three of eight Australian jurisdictions rest on search-result text, which
   this project does not accept as a source. **No Australian block can claim national coverage until they are read.**
2. **Tasmania's consumer gas emergency page is gone** (404 after a site redevelopment). If it stays gone it must be
   recorded as **SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE / NOT RELIED UPON**, like enHealth's rainwater guidance.
3. **Four Australian pages found by search but not read live** — ESV "Heating your home with gas", NSW "Using a
   gasfitter" and "Gas water heaters", WA "Servicing gas appliances". **The NSW annual and WA over-ten-years
   servicing figures come from these and must be re-read before they are quoted.**
4. **FENZ's LPG heater guidance** (NZ-10) is search-only and must be re-read live.
5. **No NZ household cylinder storage guidance exists** — only transport. A member storing a barbecue bottle at home
   has no New Zealand source to follow.
6. **No CO alarm placement distance, test interval or lifespan exists anywhere**, and the only lifespan statement is
   a varying 2–7 year range in Victorian industry guidance.
7. **AS/NZS 5601** is the genuinely national instrument in both countries and is **not freely readable**. Nothing may
   be quoted from it; it can only be named.
8. **No source in either market publishes household ventilation in measurable terms** for gas — no air changes, no
   m³/h, no vent area. Every statement is qualitative ("adequately ventilated", "a window open").
9. **No emergency indoor-use guidance exists**, and none may be written.

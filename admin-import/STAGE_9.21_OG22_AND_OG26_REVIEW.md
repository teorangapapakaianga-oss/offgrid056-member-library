## 3. The MUST-items framework (exact wording)

OG-22 uses **MoSCoW**, a standard prioritisation technique, not an OffGrid056 invention.

| Priority | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **MUST Have** | *"Critical for safety, health, or basic function. Cannot proceed without it."* | *"Buy first. Allocate budget before anything else."* |
| **SHOULD Have** | *"Important for comfort, efficiency, or resilience. Significant impact if omitted."* | *"Buy second. Include in 90-day plan."* |
| **COULD Have** | *"Desirable. Useful but not essential. Nice upgrade if budget allows."* | *"Defer to later phase or sale events."* |
| **WON'T Have (Now)** | *"Explicitly out of scope for this plan. Avoids scope creep."* | *"Record to prevent impulse buying."* |

**Section A definition:** *"Without these, your resilience plan fails or safety is compromised."*

**How an item qualifies as MUST.** *"Before adding any item to MUST, score it against these criteria"*, each
scored 1–5:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Safety Critical | Does this directly protect life or health in an emergency? |
| Time Sensitivity | How soon would failure become dangerous? (1 = months, 5 = hours) |
| Dependency Chain | Do other systems rely on this to function? |
| Cost Efficiency | Does this save or generate value within 12 months? |
| Skill Required | Can you install or operate this with current skills? (1 = no, 5 = yes) |

*"Items scoring 20+ total should be considered for MUST. Items scoring below 12 are likely COULD or WON'T."*

**How MUST is used:**

- *"Highlight the top 5 'MUST' items as your Phase 1 procurement batch."*
- *"Your MUST list becomes your Phase 1 procurement plan."*
- MUST items are broken into three purchase batches: Immediate, Month 1–2, and Month 3–6.

**Can OG-22 be the canonical source? Yes.** It is the **only** resource in the programme that defines MUST (20
uses, against 2 in OG-26 and 2 in OG-27). Its definition, *"Critical for safety, health, or basic function"*,
matches OG-26's Tier 1, *"items that protect life and basic function"*, which is why the two fit together. Nothing
needs redefining in OG-26, as you ruled.

## 8. Stored drinking water — NZ and AU (verified, awaiting your approval)

**New block, HIGH severity. The wording must vary by market:** the official amounts differ, and only NZ publishes a
home bottle-preparation method.

**NZ** — source: [Get Ready (NZ Civil Defence) — Storing water](https://getready.govt.nz/prepared/household/supplies/storing-water),
including its "Prepare containers of water" section, **read live 22 Sep 2026**.

> **Store at least three litres of drinking water per person per day, for at least three days** — nine litres per
> person. Store more if you can: hot weather and hard physical work can double what you need, children, nursing
> mothers and people who are unwell need more, and pets need water too. Some areas could be without water for
> longer; your local emergency management committee can advise how much to store.
> Use clean plastic soft-drink bottles or food-grade water containers — never containers that have held milk, and
> not glass or cardboard. Clean them with hot (not boiling) water, fill to the top with tap water, add five drops of
> plain, unscented household bleach per litre, and do not drink it for at least 30 minutes. Close the containers
> tightly, write the date on them, and store them in a cool, dark place.
> **Check stored water every six months.** If it is not clear, throw it out and refill. Keep bought bottled water
> sealed and replace it by its use-by date. Disinfect collected rainwater before drinking it, and if you are unsure
> whether water is safe, do not drink it.

**AU** — sources:

- [Get Ready Queensland — Emergency kit checklist](https://www.getready.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-12/GRQ%20Emergency%20Kit%20checklist.pdf):
  *"Water for three days – 10 litres of drinking water per person at a minimum"* and *"review contents at least
  twice a year"*
- [HealthyWA — Emergency treatment of drinking water supplies](https://www.healthywa.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Emergency-treatment-of-drinking-water-supplies):
  containers clean, covered, above ground, cool, cleaned periodically, mosquito-proof; *"Vigorous boiling for at
  least 1 minute"*

> **Store at least 10 litres of drinking water per person for three days.** Your state or territory emergency
> service may advise more for your area.
> Keep stored water in clean containers with covers, in a cool place off the ground, and make sure the containers
> are mosquito-proof. Clean the containers from time to time.
> **Check your stored water whenever you review your emergency kit — at least twice a year.** If you are not sure
> water is safe to drink, boil it vigorously for at least one minute and let it cool, or follow your state or
> territory health department's advice.

**Deliberately not stated:**

- **AU bleach ratio.** WA's ratios depend on the bleach's chlorine strength, so a single figure would be wrong for
  some products.
- **Any shelf life.**
- **Any tank size.**

Get Ready NZ blocks automated fetching (403), so it was read in the browser, as a person would. The Queensland
Health water page returned 403 and is **not** used.

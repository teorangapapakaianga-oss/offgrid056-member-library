### Why This Matters
Your property situation shapes which changes are yours to make, which may need someone else’s agreement, and which you need to check first. Knowing that before you spend or build keeps your resilience plan realistic.

This worksheet helps you record your situation and list what to check before you make changes. It does not tell you what you may or may not do. That depends on your property, your agreements and where you live, so use this worksheet to decide what to check and who to ask.

It supports your resilience plan and, if it applies to you, your off-grid plans. More independence starts with knowing what you control and what you need to confirm.

> **Before You Begin** — This worksheet is for personal planning only. It is not legal, building or professional advice, and nothing you tick or write here is a conclusion about what is allowed. Leave anything you do not know blank and come back to it.

### Step 1: Describe Your Property Situation
Answer each question separately. Tick one answer for each, or choose Other and write your own. If you are not sure, tick Not sure and come back to it.

**B. What type of dwelling is it?** ☐ Detached home ☐ Townhouse or attached home ☐ Apartment or unit ☐ Rural dwelling ☐ Other ☐ Not sure
**C. What is the property setting?** ☐ Urban or suburban ☐ Rural or lifestyle ☐ Other ☐ Not sure
**D. What is the building stage?** ☐ Existing ☐ Renovating ☐ Building new ☐ Not sure

Use your grid to write three short rules for your plan. Keep them practical. A rule is a working note, not a final answer, and you can change it once you have checked.

- **Rule 1: What I can do** — *Changes I am confident are mine to make, and that I am ready to plan.* (three lines)
